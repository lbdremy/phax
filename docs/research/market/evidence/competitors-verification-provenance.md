# Competitors, substitutes and complements: verify, review, record and govern AI-written code

Research notes, state of the market on 2026-11-25. Scope: four sub-clusters (A AI code review, B provenance / session capture / attribution, C guardrails / sandboxes / policy, D governance, regulation and evidence of demand), each compared with "the subject tool" (an Apache-2.0 local CLI for verified delegation: plan-driven phases, program-checked gates, planned-vs-touched reconciliation, acceptance tests written first, protected files, security modes, compliance + headless code review with finding ids and fates, per-phase records on a Git branch in the user's repository, cost ledger, recorded approvals).

Method notes for the report writer:

- Every evidence item below was opened in this session on 2026-11-25 through a page-fetch tool that returns an extraction of the page. Quotes are reproduced as the tool returned them; the tool caps some quotations, so a few are short fragments. Figures are exact as returned.
- Items marked **STALE** were published more than 12 months before 2026-11-25. Items marked **undated** are living pages (pricing, docs) read on the access date.
- "Vendor-run study" = the publisher sells a product that benefits from the finding. "Independent" = no product in the measured category, as far as the source shows.
- Facts seen only in search-result snippets and not opened are **not** in Evidence; they are listed under Gaps as unverified leads.
- The web-search budget of the session ran out before the last round of checks; the items this left open are listed under Gaps.
- The subject tool's own properties come from the research brief, cited as (brief), not from any web source.

### D. Governance, regulation and the evidence that creates demand

**EU AI Act** — what the text covers and when.
- The Digital Omnibus amendment is in force: Annex III high-risk obligations moved from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027, Annex I to 2 August 2028 (E-VER-77).
- Article 50(2) marking obligations: providers of systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 (E-VER-77).
- Article 12 record-keeping applies to high-risk AI systems, which "shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system" (E-VER-78); it is about the AI system's own logs, not about code that a developer wrote with an AI tool (E-VER-78).
- Article 50(2) obliges providers of AI systems "generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content" to mark outputs as machine-readable and detectable (E-VER-79); the text does not name source code (E-VER-79).
- No opened source shows the AI Act requiring an organisation to record which of its code was AI-written or how it was verified (E-VER-77, E-VER-78, E-VER-79).

**EU Cyber Resilience Act** — timeline and what it asks.
- Reporting of actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents has applied since 11 September 2026 (24-hour early warning, 72-hour notification); "The main obligations introduced by the Act will apply from 11 December 2027" (E-VER-80).
- The Commission's July 2026 guidance, as summarised by Orrick, covers due diligence on integrated components (Article 13(5)), technical documentation (Article 31) and regular testing, and does not mention AI-generated code or records of who wrote code (E-VER-81).
- The guidance defers the CRA's interplay with the AI Act and DORA to future guidance (E-VER-81).

**NIST SSDF** — in motion.
- SP 800-218 Rev. 1 (SSDF 1.2) is an Initial Public Draft dated 2025-12-17, comments closed 2026-01-30, no final listed on 2026-11-25 (E-VER-82).
- A NIST presentation of 2026-05-20 lists "AI as Code Producers", concentrating "on AI-generated code executed as the final product", and "AI as Process Participants", including "autonomous agents for build orchestration, testing, and deployment", as targeted focus areas of the SSDF work (E-VER-83).
- The practice-level wording was not read, so whether the draft asks for provenance or review records of AI-generated code is not established here (E-VER-82, E-VER-83).

**SOC 2** — audit expectation without a rule.
- The AICPA "has not published AI-specific Trust Services Criteria, AI-specific points of focus, or an authoritative AI addendum", and auditors improvise, so two companies can hold clean reports having tested different controls (E-VER-84).
- That note concerns AI systems and model deployment, not AI-written code (E-VER-84); commentary that existing change-management criteria apply unchanged to AI-initiated changes was seen only in search snippets (see Gaps).

**EU DORA (finance)**.
- Article 9(4)(e) requires that "all changes to ICT systems are recorded, tested, assessed, approved, implemented and verified in a controlled manner", with no reference to AI or to who writes the code (E-VER-87).

**FINRA (US broker-dealers)**.
- The 2026 oversight report (released 2025-12-09), as summarised by Debevoise, lists "Coding" as a GenAI use case and, for agents, points firms to "determining where human-in-the-loop oversight is required", "tracking agent actions and decisions" and "establishing guardrails or control mechanisms to constrain agent behavior" (E-VER-85).
- It also suggests "maintaining prompt and output logs for accountability and troubleshooting" and "tracking which model version was used and when" (E-VER-85).
- These are considerations in an oversight report as rendered by a law firm, not rule text (E-VER-85).

**Cyber-insurance**.
- From 1 January 2026 ISO (Verisk) generative-AI exclusion endorsements exist for commercial general liability; some cyber carriers apply "AI sublimits near 10% of overall cyber policy limits" (E-VER-86).
- Reported underwriting asks (one uncorroborated trade source) include a documented human override and logging (E-VER-86); no source opened shows a policy condition requiring human review or an audit trail of AI-written code (E-VER-86).

**Open-source project policies** (a non-regulatory form of the same demand).
- The Linux kernel and LLVM both put responsibility on a human who must review AI-generated content (E-VER-42, E-VER-45), and LLVM bans agents acting without human approval (E-VER-45).

**Incidents that drove demand**.
- PocketOS, April 2026: a Cursor agent deleted a production database and its volume backups in one API call, 30-hour outage; the account is the founder's, without an independent post-mortem (E-VER-88).
- Amazon, reported March 2026: internal documents cited "Gen-AI assisted changes" in a "trend of incidents" before the wording was removed; Amazon attributes the one AI-linked incident to "user error" (E-VER-89).
- Attacks on the agents themselves: 157 confirmed malicious skills among 98,380 in two registries (peer-reviewed, USENIX Security 2026) (E-VER-91); a May–June 2026 worm that planted `.claude/`, `.gemini/`, `.cursor/` and `.vscode/` configurations, after which GitHub disabled 73 repositories in Microsoft organisations (E-VER-90); 36.82% of 3,984 scanned skills with at least one security flaw per Snyk (E-VER-90).
- The Claude security-review action itself warns it is "not hardened against prompt injection attacks" (E-VER-22), and Claude Code Review reads its instructions from the base branch, "never from the pull request's own changes" (E-VER-19).

**Independent and vendor evidence on defect rates and the review bottleneck** (see matrix of studies in Claims).
- Volume: GitHub says more than 40% of pull requests started from an agent draft in September 2026 (E-VER-17); Sonar's survey respondents put AI at 65% of committed code (E-VER-70).
- Bottleneck: 85% agree the bottleneck moved from writing to reviewing and validating (vendor-commissioned Harris Poll, n = 1,528) (E-VER-49); 53% spend more time reviewing than creating, against 18% before AI (vendor survey, n = 1,026) (E-VER-70); median review time 5x at high AI adoption (vendor telemetry, about 22,000 developers) (E-VER-71).
- Verification is falling, not rising: "always check before committing" fell from 48% to 39% in a year (E-VER-69, E-VER-70).
- Defects, vendor-run: 1.7x issues per PR in AI-co-authored PRs (n = 470) (E-VER-67); +28% bugs per PR and 3x incidents per PR at high adoption (E-VER-71); 38% of generation tasks still introduce a vulnerability across 18 models (E-VER-66); duplication +81% and refactoring down from 21% to 3.8% of changed lines (E-VER-68).
- Defects, independent: between 7.5% and 14.8% of each assistant's commits introduce at least one static-analysis issue, 89.3% of them code smells, and 22.7% of introduced issues survive to the latest revision (302.6k commits, 6,299 repositories) (E-VER-75).
- Delivery: DORA 2025 found AI adoption no longer tied to lower throughput but still tied to instability (about 5,000 respondents; stale) (E-VER-72).
- Speed: METR's randomised trials give -4% (CI -15% to +9%) on its late-2025 cohort and call the signal unreliable (E-VER-74).
